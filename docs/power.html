One profile per machine: its hardware, the best it has measured, what its accelerators actually drew while training and serving, and where to run it. Power traces are watts from consecutive energy-counter deltas, sampled every 0.1 s by benchmark/power.py during training and analysis/power_sidecar.py during serving — one thin line per device, devices no rank bound to included, because their flat lines are where most of a single-rank node's energy goes. Aurora's whole-card counters are excluded from every total, as everywhere on this site, since they cover silicon the per-tile counters already report. Specifications are per node; sources are in the _source fields of configs/machines.json. The cross-machine comparison lives on the training page.

gemma-3-27b-it — TP=4

google/gemma-3-27b-it · TP=4 · ISL 1,024 · OSL 256 · idle floor 1,081 W

output tokens/sdynamic power

From 1 concurrent request to 32, output throughput rises 19.9× while dynamic power goes 102 W to 187 W — 1.82×. The same work costs 18.5× less energy, almost entirely by finishing sooner rather than by drawing less.

Paid for in latency: time to first token goes 290 ms to 4,362 ms and inter-token latency 92.4 ms to 130.2 ms. Tok/J is the figure to budget with, since an allocation bills for the node either way; Tok/J dyn is what the silicon did. Dynamic power is a small difference between two large numbers, so it holds only while the floor comes from this node in this job — which is why one is sampled per run rather than carried over. Given that, it is the steadier of the two: two Aurora runs on different nodes differed 4.5% in node draw and 0.07% in dynamic, because a node that idles high runs high under load too and the offset cancels.
